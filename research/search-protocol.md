# Collection method and source verification

Updated 17 September 2026. **Review type:** targeted, structured scoping review. **Language:** English. The initial searches were conducted on 16 September, with targeted additions and verification on 17 September 2026. This is not a systematic review or meta-analysis.

## Scope

The collection supports research into a verification layer for human-created/captured versus AI-generated/manipulated content, and human participation in interactions. It covers text, images, speech, video and combinations of these. Calls, meetings, posts, shorts, messages and recordings are examples; application and transport are open-ended. Privacy, safety and reliability motivate the collection.

The original search included real-versus-AI CAPTCHA design. Following the owner's clarification, that material is historical background. The current deliverable is an annotated reading collection and source documents. Architecture proposals and a previously suggested financial-workflow experiment have been removed; they are not accepted project decisions.

## Selection and extraction

Three parallel researchers covered visual/audiovisual media, voice and CAPTCHA background, and provenance/privacy. The parent covered text, messaging, official incident context and integration. The work reused verified sources from the initial review and added targeted material relevant to the clarified scope.

Searches used web-indexed primary venues and author records, including CVF, ACL Anthology, USENIX, PMLR, journal publishers, arXiv and official NIST, IETF, W3C, C2PA, FCC and government documents. Search results were discovery aids; substantive summaries rely on the cited source text. This was not a direct database export. Query and access notes are retained in the topic collections.

Included sources have a direct connection to content origin, manipulation, provenance, human participation, privacy or documented safety implications. Benchmarks and human-perception experiments are included alongside technical specifications. Standards and operational advisories are identified as such. Preprints are distinguished from peer-reviewed publications. Conference and author-manuscript versions count as the same work.

Each entry records its title, authors or issuing body, date/status, original source, a paraphrased summary and a material limitation. Linked originals supply the authors' abstracts. Empirical claims are limited to the studied task and setting. No performance ranking is inferred across incompatible datasets, and no universal human/AI classification guarantee is claimed.

## Reading copies and checks

[The document index](documents/README.md) records downloaded reading copies and original source/download URLs. Publisher or proceedings copies are preferred; open author manuscripts are used where needed and identified in the source cards. Official HTML is retained when appropriate. Downloads are checked for file type and readable content; unavailable copies are recorded rather than represented as collected. HTML snapshots may need a network connection for linked images or styling.

Bibliographic checks concern venue, year, version and publication status. Relevant primary sections are inspected for claims and limitations; this does not mean every page of every source received a systematic audit. Local checks cover document links, source-card IDs and file readability. Detector experiments and published statistical results were not independently reproduced.

## Limits of coverage

This is a curated starting collection. Search-engine indexing, accessible full texts and the selected queries limit coverage; exact screening totals and a PRISMA flow are not claimed. Current preprints may change. Application examples indicate relevance for investigation, not evidence that a method has been validated in that particular product or communication channel. Source-specific limits remain attached to each summary.
